What the surface and arrows represent

The surface follows the 95th percentile of the baroclinic-source magnitude in this 128³ cutout, smoothed over 0.8 voxels. It is a diagnostic isosurface, not a fitted shock front. Its geometry stays fixed when the color field changes.

Density, pressure, and the temperature proxy P/ρ are divided by their mean within the remnant mask. Vorticity and baroclinic-source magnitudes are divided by their RMS within that mask. These positive fields use logarithmic colors, limited to their surface 2nd–98th percentiles. Values outside the scale saturate at the end colors. Physical-unit conversions are not assumed.

Here b = ∇ρ × ∇P / ρ² is the physical baroclinic source. The signed quantity ω · b shows the baroclinic contribution to the evolution of |ω|²/2. Positive values reinforce existing vorticity, while negative values oppose it. It is one contribution to the enstrophy budget. The alignment view separates direction from magnitude.

Arrows sample the selected vector at evenly spaced locations on the surface. Equal-length arrows show direction. With magnitude scaling enabled, lengths are proportional to magnitude up to the 95th percentile of the 800 sampled vectors. Velocity is shown in the simulation frame, without subtracting remnant motion. Vorticity arrows mark rotation axes, not flow trajectories. Increasing transparency reveals arrows behind the surface but blends the surface colors.

PDFs span the full finite range of the selected quantity, including values beyond the color limits. Shell PDFs approximate area weighting by assigning one third of each triangle’s area to each vertex. Interior PDFs use equal-volume cells in the remnant mask. Each PDF integrates to one over its included samples. Logarithmic quantities have a density per unit log₁₀. Vector PDFs use the magnitude of the interpolated vector and its interior RMS. Cutaways, camera views, and arrow counts do not filter these distributions.
